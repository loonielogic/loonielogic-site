## The 30-second version

FHSA room does **not** work like TFSA room. TFSA room accrues quietly from age 18 and waits. FHSA room starts at zero and only begins accumulating in the **year the first FHSA is opened**. Every December 31 that passes without an open FHSA is **$8,000 of tax-free room gone forever**. Open one in December 2026, even with $0 in it, and 2027 starts with **$16,000** of room. Wait until January 2027 and it starts with $8,000. The 2026 deadline is weeks away.

## The numbers that matter in 2026

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Annual room | **$8,000**, starting in the year the first FHSA is opened (not the year of turning 18) |
| Lifetime limit | **$40,000** per person |
| Carry-forward | Unused room carries forward, up to **$8,000** |
| Most room in a single year | **$16,000** ($8,000 new + $8,000 carried forward) |
| Open in Dec 2026, contribute $0 | 2027 room: **$16,000** |
| Open in Jan 2027 | 2027 room: **$8,000**. The 2026 room never exists |
| Funding required to bank the room | **None**. The opening date banks it, not the first deposit |
| Form to report opening | **Schedule 15**, filed with the 2026 return, even with $0 contributed |
| Maximum lifespan | Ends at the earliest of the 15th anniversary year, the year of turning 71, or the year after the first qualifying withdrawal |
| Over-contribution tax | **1% per month** on the excess, filed on **RC728** |

## The rule, straight from the CRA

The CRA's FHSA page states it plainly: "Your FHSA participation room in the first year you open your FHSA is $8,000." Room is tied to the **opening date**, not to a birthday.

- Open in 2023: room from 2023.
- Open in 2026: the first year of room is 2026.
- Open in 2027: nothing for 2026, ever.

There is no retroactive accrual, no make-up provision, and no way to claim the years before the account was opened.

## The math that makes December urgent

The carry-forward rules make one month of delay expensive. Unused FHSA room carries forward (up to $8,000), so opening in 2026 with $0 contributed still banks the full $8,000 for 2027.

| Path | 2026 room | 2027 carry-forward | 2027 room |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.** Open in December 2026, contribute $0 | $8,000 (unused) | $8,000 | **$16,000** |
| **B.** Wait and open in January 2027 | None. It never existed | $0 | **$8,000** |

The difference is one month of waiting and **$8,000 of lifetime tax-free room**. With a lifetime cap of $40,000, that is a fifth of everything the account will ever hold, lost because of a calendar page.

Opening in December also costs nothing beyond the account itself: there is no requirement to contribute in 2026. The account can sit at $0 while the room banks.

## The pre-Dec 31 checklist

**Step 1. Confirm eligibility on the day of opening.** The requirements:

- **Age:** 18 or older (some issuers require 19 in provinces where that is the age of majority), and 71 or younger on December 31 of the year of opening.
- **Residency:** a resident of Canada.
- **First-time buyer test:** it looks at where the person **lived**, not just what they owned. In the current calendar year before opening, and in the four calendar years before that, the person must not have lived, as a principal residence, in a home they owned or jointly owned, or that their spouse or common-law partner (at the time of opening) owned or jointly owned.
- Owning a property never lived in, such as a rental, does **not** disqualify on its own.

**Step 2. Pick an issuer.** Banks, credit unions, brokerages, and robo-advisors offer FHSAs. The choice comes down to what the account will hold: a savings-account FHSA is the simple option; a self-directed FHSA can hold ETFs or GICs, much like an RRSP.

**Step 3. Open the account in 2026, even with $0.** The opening date is what banks the room. Waiting for a bonus, a promotion, or a January lump sum does not bank anything. Funding can come later; the room is secured by the opening date, not the first deposit. Issuer processing can take a few business days, so leave a buffer before December 31.

**Step 4. File Schedule 15 with the 2026 tax return.** The CRA requires reporting the opening of a first FHSA for the year it was opened, even if nothing was contributed. This is how the CRA's records learn the account exists, and how the room shows up on the notice of assessment. No Schedule 15, no paper trail.

**Step 5. Plan 2027 contributions instead of rushing in 2026.** Room for 2027 is **$16,000** for an account opened in 2026 with nothing contributed. Contributions made in January 2027 count toward 2027, so there is no need to fund the account in December. Contributions can be spaced through the year to fit cash flow.

**Step 6. The deduction can wait for a higher-income year.** FHSA contributions are deductible in the year made or can be carried forward to a later year, like an RRSP. Contributing in 2026 and deducting in 2027 (for example, ahead of an expected raise) is allowed. The room and the deduction are separate levers.

## Traps that eat the deadline

1. **The TFSA mental model.** TFSA room starts at 18 and waits. FHSA room starts at opening and does not. Treating the FHSA like a TFSA is the single most expensive assumption in this account.
2. **The 15-year clock starts at opening too.** The maximum participation period begins in the year the first FHSA is opened and ends at the earliest of: the 15th anniversary year, the year of turning 71, or the year after the first qualifying withdrawal. For a 25-year-old this is a non-issue. For someone opening at 60, the clock is ticking from day one.
3. **The spouse test at opening.** With a spouse or common-law partner at the time of opening, that partner's owned-and-lived-in homes count against first-time-buyer status for opening, even if the applicant's name was never on the title. Both histories matter before the application starts.
4. **The over-contribution tax.** Contributions plus RRSP transfers that exceed the year's room attract **1% per month** on the excess (filed on **RC728**). With December openings, a common mistake is contributing the full $8,000 in late December and then contributing again in January before confirming the 2027 room calculation. Knowing the room before each contribution avoids it.

## FAQ

**Does money have to go in during 2026?** No. Opening banks the room; funding is separate. An FHSA opened in December 2026 with $0 contributed gives $8,000 of carry-forward into 2027, for $16,000 of total 2027 room.

**Can both partners in a couple do this?** Yes. Each eligible person gets their own $8,000 annual and $40,000 lifetime limits, so a couple can bank up to **$80,000** of room toward the same first home.

**What if the home purchase never happens?** The FHSA has an exit ramp: the balance can be transferred to an RRSP or RRIF tax-free (without using any RRSP room), or withdrawn as taxable income. The account does not punish a change of plans, though it must close by the end of the 15th year.

**What if an FHSA was already opened in a previous year?** No urgency: room has been accruing since the opening year. The notice of assessment shows current room and carry-forward.
