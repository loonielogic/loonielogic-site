one_idea: >-
  When you leave savings alone, they can grow on their own. That growth has a

Last spring the loon found two lost loonies on the dock. She had a choice. Spend them on the glittering mirror stall right now, or tuck them into a safe nest box and see what happened. She chose the nest box.

A season passed. When she peeked inside, there were still two loonies, but there was also a tiny third coin beside them, like a surprise baby. The bank she kept them with had added a thank-you gift. The grown-up word for the gift is interest. Interest means: the bank says thank you for leaving your money with us by giving you a little extra.

Then the loon learned the best part. She left the two loonies AND the baby coin in the box. Next season, the gift was a little bigger, because now there were three coins working instead of two. Her money was making money, and that money was making money. That is the whole secret. Spend money and it is gone. Save money and it can quietly get bigger all by itself.

Now imagine a kid story with round numbers (this is pretend, just to show the idea). You save $100 and put it somewhere safe. After a while, a $5 thank-you gift appears, so you have $105. Then the gift starts coming on $105 instead of $100, so it gets a little bigger each time. Nobody is doing any work. The money is just... growing. That is why patient savers end up with more than people who spend everything right away.

There is one more thing to know, and the loon wishes someone had told her earlier: the trick only works if you do not spend the savings. The moment the box opens and the coins go to the mirror stall, the growing stops. Growth rewards patience. That is the price of the prize, and it is a fair price.
