## The math behind the verdict

Take a beginner keeping a $5,000 working balance:

| Account | Rate | Interest per year on $5,000 |
|---|---|---|
| EQ Bank (with $2,000+/month pay deposit) | 2.75% | $137.50 |
| Wealthsimple Chequing (base) | 1.25% | $62.50 |
| EQ Bank (no pay deposit) | 1.00% | $50.00 |
| Simplii, Tangerine, Neo, CIBC | Not yet verified | Not yet verified |

That is the whole argument for EQ: $137.50 a year for holding money you were going to hold anyway. It is also the case for Wealthsimple when your pay can't move: $62.50 beats EQ's $50 base on the same balance.

The one-time bonuses are real, but do the per-year math: <PromoCopy figure="simplii.bonus_2026" fallback="a welcome bonus pays once, while the interest gap pays every year you hold the balance.">Simplii's $350 bonus is equivalent to about two and a half years of EQ interest on that balance, paid once.</PromoCopy>

Now the big-bank default many beginners inherit from their parents. We haven't verified current big-bank fees or waiver minimums, so we don't quote them. The principle holds at any fee: $5,000 parked only to waive a monthly fee still gives up the interest it could earn elsewhere, which is $50 to $137.50 a year at EQ's rates.

One more honest note on EQ: it is a digital-only bank, so there are no branches. Anyone who wants in-person service is choosing a different kind of bank.

## The expensive mistakes beginners actually make

1. **Paying a monthly fee out of inertia.** An account opened years ago by a parent can carry a monthly fee nobody questions. Every month it stays open is money out the door, and moving to a no-fee account online is quick.
2. **Parking $5,000 to waive a fee while keeping no emergency fund.** The waived fee feels like a win; the math says you gave up $50 to $137.50 a year in interest and locked up your safety net. Never optimize for fee waivers ahead of liquidity.
3. **Confusing a savings promo rate with the ongoing rate.** A high promotional rate for a few months is marketing. What matters is the rate after the promo ends. Check the base rate, not the banner.
4. **Believing "no-fee" means "no fees."** Certified cheques, bank drafts, stop payments, and foreign transaction fees still exist at most no-fee banks. Neo's free tier, for one, adds a $4.99 NSF fee from January 1, 2027.

## The 60-second quiz: your account

Work top to bottom. The first question that applies to you ends the quiz.

1. **Does your paycheque (at least $2,000 a month) go into this account?** Yes → **EQ Bank Personal Account**. 2.75% on your balance: $137.50 a year on $5,000.
2. **Are you new to Canada with no Canadian pay history yet?** Yes → **Simplii No Fee Chequing**. Its New to Canada banking program is built for people arriving without Canadian pay history. Once your pay starts, reconsider EQ.
3. **Is a one-time welcome bonus what moves you, and is the offer still live?** <PromoCopy figure="simplii.bonus_2026" fallback="Promo bonuses have expired or changed since this page was verified: check the comparison table above for the current offers.">Yes → **Simplii** pays $350, the biggest payout, for four months of $100+ monthly direct deposits; the offer runs to January 31, 2027 (re-verified live October 1, 2026).</PromoCopy> <PromoCopy figure="tangerine.payroll_250">Tangerine's $250 payroll bonus runs until October 31, 2026 as the second bonus play.</PromoCopy> Never open an account for a bonus you haven't confirmed is live today.
4. **Do you spend abroad or shop cross-border online often?** Yes → **Wealthsimple**. Its Visa card charges 0% on foreign transactions ($240 a year on Core, $0 at Premium and Generation), in the same app as Wealthsimple Chequing.
5. **Are you under 25 and want a branch plus a student credit card path?** Yes → **CIBC Smart Start**. We haven't verified its age limit or what it becomes later: check both on CIBC's site and set a calendar reminder for the date it changes.
6. **Do you want cashback on gas and groceries?** Yes → **Neo Chequing**. Read the 2027 fees before you commit: from January 1, 2027 the free tier charges $1 per e-Transfer sent and $1.50 per ATM withdrawal.

No to all of the above, or genuinely unsure? **Default to the EQ Bank Personal Account if your pay can go there later**, for the 2.75% rate. If your pay will never go there, Wealthsimple Chequing's 1.25% base beats EQ's 1.00% on the same balance.
