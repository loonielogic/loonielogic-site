## The 30-second version

**Wealthsimple is the default pick for most beginners.** It puts chequing, investing, a credit card, tax filing, and crypto in one app, with $0 commissions on stocks and ETFs. **Questrade wins in two situations:** your TFSA or RRSP will hold mostly US stocks or ETFs (Questrade's free dual-currency accounts sidestep the 1.5% conversion fee both charge), or you want the cheapest hands-off managed portfolio (Questwealth's fee is roughly half of Wealthsimple's, confirmed on Questrade's own pricing page). Stock and ETF commissions are $0 at both, so "free trading" doesn't decide this. **Currency conversion and the fee on managed money do.**

**Who pays us here:** nobody, today. Questrade runs an open affiliate program that pays publishers $50 to $250 per funded account; we have not joined it. Wealthsimple has no public affiliate signup. So we earn nothing from either link on this page, and if that ever changes, the disclosure at the top changes first and this verdict does not.

## The math behind the verdict

Three worked comparisons on 2026 numbers.

### 1. Managed money: Questwealth's fee is roughly half

A $50,000 hands-off portfolio:

| | Wealthsimple (Core) | Questwealth |
|---|---|---|
| Management fee | 0.50% = $250/year | 0.25% = $125/year |
| Underlying fund fees (MER) | 0.12-0.15% = $60-$75/year | 0.09-0.12% = $45-$60/year (official Questrade pricing page, verified October 1, 2026) |
| **All-in** | **$310-$325/year** | **$170-$185/year** |

You save about $125-$155 a year at Questrade on the same $50,000. At $100K (where Wealthsimple drops to 0.40% at Premium and Questwealth to 0.20%), the all-in gap is about $200-$260 a year. Wealthsimple counters with no minimum; Questwealth needs $250 to start. If "set it and forget it" is the whole plan, the fee gap is the deciding number.

### 2. US-dollar investing: Questrade's free dual-currency is the quiet killer feature

Both charge 1.5% to convert CAD to USD directly. On a $25,000 conversion for US stocks inside a TFSA, that's **$375 gone in one click**. The workarounds:

- **Questrade:** hold USD directly in the registered account for free, or journal shares for $9.95. No monthly fee anywhere in the picture. Questrade Plus ($19.99/month) makes journaling unlimited, but at $239.88 a year it takes about **24 journals a year** to pay for itself, so most beginners don't need it.
- **Wealthsimple:** Norbert's Gambit costs $9.95 plus tax per journal, but it requires a USD account, and that USD account is **$10/month ($120/year)** on Core, free only from Premium ($100K+ in assets).

Put simply: a beginner who buys US ETFs inside a TFSA saves roughly $120 a year at Questrade by skipping the USD-account fee, and avoids the 1.5% conversion with $9.95 journaling. Wealthsimple's gambit narrows the gap, but the USD-account fee is the leak that hasn't closed.

### 3. Margin: Wealthsimple charges almost half what Questrade does

| | Wealthsimple (Core) | Questrade (non-registered, under $100K) |
|---|---|---|
| CAD margin rate | 4.95% | 8.70% (CAD prime 4.45% + 4.25%) |
| USD margin rate | 7.50% | 11.50% (USD prime 7.00% + 4.50%) |
| Cost of a $20,000 CAD debit for a year | $990 | $1,740 |

That is a **$750-a-year gap** on a $20,000 balance. Margin is not a beginner product, and borrowing to invest is how beginners become cautionary tales, but the inversion is worth naming: the "beginner" brand is cheaper to borrow from than the "active trader" brand.

## When Questrade wins instead

Wealthsimple is the default, but Questrade is the right first call in four situations:

1. **Your TFSA is full of US stocks and ETFs.** Free dual-currency inside registered accounts is Questrade's single biggest structural advantage. No $120-a-year USD-account fee, no forced conversions.
2. **You want a robo portfolio and care about the fee.** Questwealth at 0.25%/0.20% is roughly half of Wealthsimple's 0.5%/0.4%, confirmed on Questrade's own pricing page. On $100,000 that's about $200-$260 a year all-in, every year.
3. **You plan to outgrow beginner tools.** CAD options, forex and CFDs, pre-IPO access, Custom Indexing, mutual funds, and bonds and GICs all exist at Questrade. Wealthsimple's shelf is simpler by design. Starting at Questrade means never switching platforms later.
4. **You want brokerage and banking kept apart.** Questrade has no chequing account or credit card, which some people prefer: the money you invest never sits one tap away from the money you spend.

## The expensive mistakes (the ways Canadians actually lose money here)

1. **Paying 1.5% on every US buy.** This is the biggest beginner leak in Canadian investing. Converting $25,000 directly for US ETFs costs $375. Journaling costs $9.95 at Questrade. Learn how the gambit works before the first US purchase, wherever the account is.
2. **Paying $10/month for a Wealthsimple USD account you barely use.** Holding US dollars year-round for frequent US trading can justify the fee. Converting once a year doesn't: the gambit is cheaper. Core clients with small USD balances pay the most and get the least.
3. **Treating the 1% Match as a reason to switch.** <PromoCopy figure="ws.match_1pct" fallback="A transfer bonus is nice, but match bonuses are typically paid out over time and can shrink if you withdraw.">A $1,000 bonus on a $100,000 transfer is nice, but it is paid over two years and shrinks if you withdraw.</PromoCopy> Switch because the platform fits, not because of the bonus calendar.
4. **Assuming "free trading" means zero cost.** ECN fees can still attach to orders that remove liquidity at Questrade, Wealthsimple's 1.5% conversion fee sits outside "commissions," and exercising an option costs $24.95 at Questrade. Read the fee schedule, not the headline.

## The 60-second quiz: your broker

Work top to bottom. The first question that applies to you ends the quiz.

1. **Do you want chequing, investing, a credit card, tax filing, and crypto under one login?** Yes → **Wealthsimple**. Nothing at Questrade compares; it is brokerage-only.
2. **Will your TFSA or RRSP hold mostly US stocks or ETFs?** Yes → **Questrade**. Free dual-currency inside registered accounts ends the 1.5% conversion drag. On a $25,000 US position that is $375 you never pay.
3. **Do you want the cheapest hands-off portfolio and "set it and forget it"?** Yes → **Questrade**. Questwealth at 0.25%/0.20% is roughly half Wealthsimple's 0.5%/0.4% managed fee: about $125 to $155 a year less on $50,000, all-in (official Questwealth pricing page, verified October 1, 2026).
4. **Do you want to trade crypto alongside stocks?** Yes → **Wealthsimple**. Questrade doesn't offer crypto. Wealthsimple charges a flat 0.5% plus spread on market orders; limit orders carry no spread.
5. **Do you file your own taxes?** Yes → **Wealthsimple**. Pay-what-you-want Basic filing lives inside the same app as your money.
6. **Do you expect to want advanced tools within two years (CAD options, forex, pre-IPO, mutual funds, bonds and GICs)?** Yes → **Questrade**. Wealthsimple's shelf is simpler by design; starting at Questrade means never switching platforms later.

No to all of the above, or genuinely unsure? **Default to Wealthsimple.** For a beginner who invests mostly in Canadian securities and wants one app that does everything, simplicity beats the last dollar of fees. If US holdings grow later, Questrade can sit alongside it, and the $9.95 journaling trick works the moment it does.
